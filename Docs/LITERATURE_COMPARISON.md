# Closest-work comparison and contribution checkpoint

Checked: 2026-09-27. Targeted comparison of four primary works, not a systematic
review, exhaustive novelty search or final manuscript. No numerical calibration
inputs were imported. The authors continue to lead broader source collection.

## 1. Committee assessment

Research judgment: the existing framework is internally coherent, but the
current affine crossings and spending-share identity do not, by themselves,
establish a distinct mathematical contribution. Supplier-switching frictions,
costly factor adjustment and task automation are established research areas.
The AI framing also overlaps contemporary political-economy work; calling the
mechanism AI lock-in is not enough to establish novelty.

The defensible current position is a conditional exposure and human-restoration
framework. A stronger theory-paper claim needs a particular result that the
closest models do not already deliver. This check does not establish that such
a result is absent from the literature.

## 2. Primary-source register and reading limits

### L1. Switching costs and supplier competition

Joseph Farrell and Paul Klemperer, 2007, *Coordination and Lock-In: Competition
with Switching Costs and Network Effects*, Handbook of Industrial Organization,
volume 3, pages 1967-2072, DOI 10.1016/S1573-448X(06)03031-7.

[Published chapter](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/handbook/pii/S1573448X06030317);
[May 2006 author draft](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9n26k7v1/qt9n26k7v1.pdf).
The dated author draft was the accessible text examined, particularly sections
2.1 and 2.3.1, not a full reading of the final chapter.

The review treats seller-specific investment, learning and transaction costs
as switching frictions, and discusses introductory pricing followed by prices
charged to locked-in customers when future prices cannot be committed to.
These mechanisms are direct antecedents to a claim that dependence can weaken
substitution discipline. They do not justify treating our cost threshold as
a firm's equilibrium price.

### L2. Labour adjustment and delayed input substitution

Daniel S. Hamermesh and Gerard A. Pfann, 1996, *Adjustment Costs in Factor
Demand*, Journal of Economic Literature 34(3), pages 1264-1292.

[Journal record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2729502);
[article PDF hosted by Athens University of Economics and Business](https://eclass.aueb.gr/modules/document/file.php/LOXR338/%CE%B1%CF%81%CE%B8%CF%81%CE%B1%20%CE%B3%CE%B9%CE%B1%20adjustment%20costs%20etc/adjustment_costs_factor_demand.pdf).
Examined introduction and sections 2-3, with targeted checks of the aggregation
discussion; this was not a comprehensive re-analysis of its empirical studies.

The paper distinguishes ongoing input costs from costs of adjustment,
including search, training and production disruption. It considers different
adjustment-cost structures and difficulties of aggregation. It supports
separating ordinary wages from rebuilding burdens, but supplies no current
AI-era institutional reconstruction bill, ramp or universal F(K) law.

### L3. Task automation and labour demand

Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo, 2019, *Automation and New Tasks: How
Technology Displaces and Reinstates Labor*, Journal of Economic Perspectives
33(2), pages 3-30, DOI 10.1257/jep.33.2.3.

[Official article and abstract](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.33.2.3).
The publisher abstract and bibliographic record were examined; a full
equation-by-equation comparison remains for the authors.

Its framework allocates tasks between capital and labour, contrasting
automation's displacement effect with the labour-demand effect of new tasks.
This is an antecedent for task-based production analysis. Our supplied
restoration paths concern replacing the affected workload with humans,
not a forecast of employment or a model of endogenous new-task creation.

### L4. Contemporary AI, infrastructure dependence and human capital

Gerbrand Tholen, 2026, *From human capital to asset ownership: AI as rentier
asset*, Critical Sociology, OnlineFirst June 23, 2026,
DOI 10.1177/08969205261461604.

[Open-access publisher article](https://doi.org/10.1177/08969205261461604).
Examined the abstract, introduction, infrastructure/access-control discussion
and conclusion. Its cited market statistics were not independently verified
and are not used as calibration here.

The article connects AI ownership and access control with infrastructure
dependence and the position of university-educated workers. This is close
conceptual overlap with our motivating question. Our conditional calculations
are not evidence that its broader rent-extraction claims hold. The reading
does not establish that a formal restoration threshold is unique to our work.

## 3. What overlaps, and what our framework actually supplies

This matrix is our interpretation of the reviewed material, not a claim that
the papers exhaust each literature or omit every possible related mechanism.

| Reference | Closest overlap | Our current object | Consequence for positioning |

## 4. Recommended research positioning

Working title:

> AI Supplier Dependence and the Cost of Restoring Human Production:
> A Conditional Exposure Framework

Working contribution description, not a novelty claim:

> We formalize the cost and feasibility of returning AI-supplied output to
> humans over a common horizon, separating immediate spending exposure from
> human-restoration and rival-provider outside options. We identify the
> conditions under which retained human capacity changes substitution
> thresholds and the conditions under which rival access limits that effect.
> Hypothetical company applications show how matched internal inputs and
> separate institutional assumptions enter the calculation.

The distinguishing emphasis to investigate is the human productive-capacity
outside option, not simply migration between AI vendors. Retained capability,
immediately available output, institutional rebuilding and transition bills
are separate quantities. Their separation improves accounting, but does not
by itself establish a new economic theory.

## 5. Contribution filter

| Proposed claim | Current assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Price exposure increases with affected spending | Accounting identity under a fixed mix |
| Positive rebuilding outlays widen a simple reversal gap | Built into the stated cost comparison |
| Costly exit permits weaker supplier discipline | Strong overlap with switching-cost research |
| Cheap qualified rivals can limit a restoration threshold | Useful boundary result, not established as original |
| Longer rebuilding time always implies stronger monopoly | Unsupported; cost roots, timing, feasibility and strategic pricing differ |
| A shared institutional capacity limit can defeat independent sector substitution plans | Conditional common-price exposure floor now derived in SHARED_CAPACITY_BOUND.md; elementary reasoning and originality still require closest-model comparison |
| The model estimates economy-wide monopoly power | Unsupported without representative coverage and separate market-power identification |

For a theory-focused submission, more hypothetical grids are not the missing
ingredient. The candidate is a specific bound on unavoidable residual
exposure when jointly available institutional capacity cannot supply all
requested human replacement and common upstream shocks also reprice rivals.
The analytical [shared-capacity bound](SHARED_CAPACITY_BOUND.md) is now derived
and implemented with hypothetical examples. It is not an allocation optimizer,
an expansion into endogenous workforce policy, or an established novel result.

## 6. Next bounded research checkpoint

The authors should make a closer comparison around the candidate result:

1. For each closely related model, record its state variables, timing,
   outside-option definition, capacity constraints and whether prices are
   endogenous.
2. Map one of our proposed propositions to its nearest existing result.
   Identify the precise extra assumption and changed conclusion.
3. Search specifically for buyer capability erosion, reconstruction of
   productive capacity, common-input supplier dependence and shared human
   capacity. Include human-capital/knowledge recovery and hold-up literature;
   those are not fully reviewed here.
4. Retain a theoretical extension only if it changes a bound, feasible set
   or comparative static rather than renaming a familiar switching bill.
5. If no distinct theoretical result survives, narrow the contribution to
   a transparent applied exposure framework; an empirical application would
   then need appropriate evidence rather than solely hypothetical examples.

No new benchmark runs, employment forecasts, causal layoff study or unrestricted
monopoly-pricing assumption follows from this checkpoint.

Closest-work comparison: in progress. Four anchor sources have traceable

still need deeper comparison. Current calculations remain valid under their
stated assumptions. Originality and publication readiness remain unresolved.
